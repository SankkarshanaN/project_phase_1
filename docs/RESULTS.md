# Measured Results — Dataset v2

Generated by `scripts/generate_report_figures.py`, `scripts/evaluate_prediction.py`
and `scripts/sweep_shadow_tolerance.py`, run against the **full 15,420-frame
`data/raw_v2` dataset**. Every number below is measured and reproducible.

Figures: `results/figures/`. Raw numbers: `results/metrics.json`,
`results/shadow_tolerance_sweep.json`.

> **These numbers supersede every figure from the v1 dataset.** In particular the
> previously reported **99.7%** validation accuracy came from a random per-crop
> train/validation split, which placed near-duplicate crops of the same object on
> both sides of the boundary. The honest figure on a split grouped by episode is
> **94.8%**. See "What changed from v1" below.

## 1. Dataset

**15,420 frames across 189 distinct episodes**, all clear-day
(`results/figures/dataset_composition.png`):

| Scenario | Frames | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `urban_crossing` (roaming ego) | 5,925 | Flagship: ego drives the city and *encounters* occlusions |
| `blindspot_cutin` | 3,615 | Vehicle merges from the rear-lateral blind spot |
| `multi_occlusion` | 2,940 | Two occluders, two pedestrians |
| `occluded_pedestrian_crossing` | 2,940 | Single staged occluder |

Every frame carries an RGB image, YOLO-format labels for camera-observable
actors, and an `.npz` holding the complete per-object record — including
**amodal boxes for fully hidden actors**, the ground-truth occlusion and
true-occupancy grids, radar returns, ego pose and velocity, and the simulation
timestamp.

### Occlusion is graded, not binary

| Tier | Observations | Share |
|---|---|---|
| OCCLUDED (visibility < 0.05) | 20,698 | 38.7% |
| PARTIAL (0.05–0.65) | 5,567 | 10.4% |
| VISIBLE (> 0.65) | 27,232 | 50.9% |

Visibility is measured by projecting a dense lattice of points sampled over each
actor's bounding-box surface and depth-testing each one, so it is a continuous
fraction: **741 distinct values** occur among partially-visible observations, and
11,072 of 11,593 fall off the coarse k/8 lattice the previous 8-corner measure
could produce. `obj_truncation` is recorded separately from `obj_visibility`,
because an actor at the frame edge is truncated, not occluded.

**103 of 189 episodes contain both a fully hidden and a clearly visible
observation of the same VRU** — the emergence event the project is about.

### The ego genuinely drives

Over the 5,925 roaming-ego frames: mean speed **5.84 m/s**, stationary for only
**18%** of ticks (red lights and traffic). This is checked automatically by
`scripts/check_dataset.py`, for reasons given below.

### Crossing-intent ground truth

`scripts/label_crossing_intent.py` replays each episode and marks, retrospectively
and therefore exactly, whether each VRU entered the ego's corridor within 3 s:

- **18,595 VRU observations**, 37.0% positive overall
- **53.9% positive** among the 11,549 observations within 12 m laterally and 40 m
  ahead — the population a crossing predictor would actually be asked about

The two figures differ because a roaming ego in a populated city sees many
pedestrians strolling a pavement tens of metres to one side. They are trivially
negative and dilute the raw rate; quoting only the raw 37% understates how
balanced the actual prediction task is.

## 2. Evidential confidence scorer

Dirichlet head (Sensoy et al. 2018) with annealed-KL loss, over 64×64 crops,
three classes (vehicle / pedestrian / background).

**Split grouped by episode: 161 train / 28 validation episodes (50,904 / 11,624
crops).** No frame from a validation episode appears in training.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| **Reported validation accuracy** | **0.948 ± 0.004** |
| Best single epoch | 0.952 (epoch 15) |
| Across-epoch std over all 15 epochs | 0.056 |

The reported figure is the **mean of the final five epochs**, not the best epoch.
Validation accuracy on a grouped split swings genuinely — it dipped to 0.753 at
epoch 5 before recovering — and with 28 held-out episodes, picking the maximum of
15 noisy measurements selects on noise. The gap is small here (0.948 vs 0.952) but
the practice matters more than the gap.

Confusion matrix over 11,624 held-out crops
(`results/figures/confusion_matrix.png`):

|  | pred vehicle | pred pedestrian | pred background |
|---|---|---|---|
| **true vehicle** | 4,538 | 191 | 213 |
| **true pedestrian** | 24 | 1,422 | 60 |
| **true background** | 48 | 18 | 5,110 |

### Uncertainty is calibrated

`results/figures/uncertainty_calibration.png`:

| Uncertainty bin | Accuracy | n |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.985 | 10,207 |
| 0.3 | 0.804 | 812 |
| 0.5 | 0.734 | 207 |
| 0.7 | 0.624 | 93 |
| 0.9 | 0.511 | 305 |

Accuracy falls monotonically from 98.5% to 51.1% as the head's own uncertainty
rises — the head knows when it does not know. **On the v1 dataset this curve was
flat**, and the reason was a data defect rather than a modelling one: the collector
discarded every box scoring `visibility < 0.15`, so the hard cases that should
have populated the high-uncertainty bins were deleted before training ever saw
them.

The head is also measurably more uncertain on partial glimpses, which is the
behaviour the design intends:

| Tier of the cropped object | Mean uncertainty | p90 |
|---|---|---|
| PARTIAL | **0.269** | 0.867 |
| VISIBLE | 0.133 | 0.236 |

(Fully OCCLUDED objects contribute no crops — there are no pixels to crop.)

## 3. Three-state occlusion detector (BEV)

Runtime path only: MiDaS relative disparity + radar, never CARLA's depth buffer.
Validated against the ground-truth grid over 200 sampled frames (80,000 cells).

At the default `shadow_tolerance = 0.12`:

| Metric (OCCLUDED class) | Value |
|---|---|
| Precision | 0.888 |
| Recall | 0.629 |
| F1 | 0.736 |
| Overall cell agreement (4 states) | 0.913 |

Per-scenario agreement: `blindspot_cutin` 0.932, `occluded_pedestrian_crossing`
0.922, `multi_occlusion` 0.907, `urban_crossing` 0.900.

### What fixed it: stop marching, fit the ground

This component measured **recall 0.259** until a defect was found in how it
propagated shadow. The original implementation walked outward cell by cell,
maintaining a running expectation of what bare ground should read and flagging
cells that read nearer than the trend.

Two things were wrong with that, and the second is the one that mattered:

- **It marched along the wrong lines.** It walked columns of constant *lateral
  index* — lines parallel to the ego's axis — where occlusion propagates along
  *rays from the camera*, which fan outward. Only the centreline column is
  both.
- **It marched at all.** Every marched track spends its first cell seeding the
  trend, and a seed cell can never be flagged. This dominates: switching to
  ray-correct marching with 40 angular bins instead of 20 columns *doubled* the
  seed count and dropped recall from 0.408 to 0.297. The geometry fix made
  things worse, because it bought correctness at the cost of more seeds.

The fix removes propagation entirely. `_ground_profile` fits what unoccluded
ground reads as a function of range, once per frame, by taking a low quantile of
disparity within each range ring — obstacles read *nearer* than ground, so they
sit in the upper tail and a low quantile estimates bare road robustly. Each cell
is then tested against its own range's ground level, with no seeds and no
ordering.

That mirrors how the ground truth is computed: `BevProjector.compute_labels`
compares the depth buffer against each cell's distance, per cell, with no
propagation. MiDaS gives relative inverse disparity with no metric scale, so a
cell cannot be depth-tested directly — but fitting the fall-off per frame
recovers the same comparison.

| | Column march | **Ground profile** |
|---|---|---|
| Recall @ default 0.12 | 0.259 | **0.629** |
| Precision @ default | 0.959 | 0.888 |
| F1 @ default | 0.408 | **0.736** |
| Cell agreement | 0.854 | **0.913** |
| Best F1 across the sweep | 0.459 | **0.718** |
| Maximum achievable recall | 0.408 | **0.854** |

### The threshold sweep

`scripts/sweep_shadow_tolerance.py`, 150 frames, 60,000 cells per setting
(`results/figures/shadow_tolerance_sweep.png`):

| `shadow_tolerance` | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | 0.483 | **0.854** | 0.616 |
| 0.04 | 0.577 | 0.794 | 0.668 |
| 0.06 | 0.666 | 0.744 | 0.703 |
| 0.08 | 0.748 | 0.691 | **0.718** |
| **0.12 (default)** | 0.873 | 0.607 | 0.716 |
| 0.16 | 0.931 | 0.537 | 0.681 |
| 0.22 | 0.958 | 0.346 | 0.508 |
| 0.30 | 0.981 | 0.143 | 0.250 |
| 0.40 | 0.995 | 0.039 | 0.075 |

The default stays at **0.12** rather than moving to the best-F1 0.08: F1 differs
by 0.002, while precision is 0.873 against 0.748. For a signal feeding a braking
decision, false alarms cost more than the F1 difference is worth.

The detector still under-detects — it misses roughly two in five occluded cells
at the default, and about one in seven at the recall-maximising end. That remains
the honest characterisation, and under-detection is the defensible failure
direction, but it is no longer the pipeline's bottleneck.

**Method note worth carrying into the report.** The sweep was originally run to
decide whether low recall was an operating-point choice or a property of the
detector, and it answered "the detector" — recall plateaued at 0.408 across the
entire threshold range. That answer was correct and still misleading: a plateau
means no *threshold* helps, not that no *algorithm* does. The same sweep, re-run
after the algorithm changed, now tops out at 0.854.

## 4. Tracking and sensor fusion — the ablation

EKF over `[x, y, vx, vy]` in the ego frame; measurement `[bearing, range,
range_rate]`. Camera supplies bearing, radar supplies range and range-rate.

### Compare modes on shared observations, not aggregate means

Each configuration holds tracks on a **different subset** of actors — 3,158
moving observations for camera-only, 2,248 for radar-only, 3,185 for fused, with
only **578 tracked by all three**. Aggregate means are therefore computed over
different populations of different difficulty, and comparing them directly
reverses the conclusion:

| | camera | radar | fused |
|---|---|---|---|
| v_lat MAE, **unpaired** (own subset) | 0.973 | 1.528 | 1.038 ← *looks worse* |
| v_lat MAE, **paired** (shared 578 obs) | 0.865 | 1.599 | **0.711** ← *is better* |

The paired comparison is the ablation's headline:

| sensors | **v_lat MAE (m/s)** | v_fwd MAE (m/s) | position error (m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| camera only | 0.865 | 0.976 | 2.279 |
| radar only | 1.599 | **0.161** | 2.661 |
| **fused** | **0.711** | 0.217 | **2.276** |

This is the physical argument for fusion, measured:

- **Radar is 6× better at forward velocity** (0.161 vs 0.976). Doppler measures
  the radial component directly.
- **Radar is 2.2× worse at lateral velocity** (1.599 vs 0.711). A pedestrian
  crossing your path has near-zero radial velocity, so the component that decides
  the outcome is precisely the one radar is blindest to.
- **Fusion beats both** on lateral velocity — 18% better than camera-only and 56%
  better than radar-only — because lateral speed ≈ range (radar) × d(bearing)/dt
  (camera track). Neither sensor can supply both factors.

The unpaired table is still reported, because how many objects a configuration can
hold a track on at all is a real property of it. It is not an estimation-accuracy
comparison and should not be quoted as one.

## 5. Crossing-intent prediction

`perception/intent.py` propagates the EKF state and covariance forward and
integrates the probability of entering the ego's corridor within 3 s.

| sensors | n | AUC | Precision | Recall | **F1** | Lead time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| camera only | 3,158 | 0.880 | 0.908 | 0.693 | 0.786 | 2.46 s |
| radar only | 2,248 | 0.648 | 0.917 | **0.014** | 0.028 | 1.93 s |
| **fused** | 3,185 | 0.867 | 0.892 | **0.834** | **0.862** | 2.32 s |

Radar-only achieves 0.014 recall: it almost never predicts a crossing at all,
which is Doppler blindness expressed as a task metric rather than an error bar.
Fusion buys **+14 points of recall over camera-only for 1.6 points of precision**,
and warns on average **2.32 s before corridor entry**.

Broken down by how well the pedestrian was seen:

| Tier at prediction time | n | AUC |
|---|---|---|
| OCCLUDED | 251 | 0.660 |
| PARTIAL | 158 | 0.767 |
| VISIBLE | 2,776 | 0.889 |

Prediction degrades gracefully rather than failing: even with the pedestrian
**fully hidden** and the track coasting on dead reckoning alone, AUC is 0.660 —
well above chance.

### The evidential coupling: an honest null result

Feeding classifier uncertainty into the prediction did **not** move aggregate
metrics (F1 0.862 both with and without; recall 0.834 → 0.835). The reason is
measurable and is not that the mechanism fails:

- The head is more uncertain on partial glimpses exactly as intended (mean 0.269
  vs 0.133 — see §2).
- But only **75 of 3,547 scored crops are PARTIAL**, and 85.5% of all crops score
  below 0.2 uncertainty. There is almost nothing for a caution mechanism to act
  on, so it cannot shift an aggregate.

Report it that way. "Uncertainty-aware prediction made no difference" is the wrong
summary; "the mechanism behaves correctly but the operating regime where it
matters is 2% of observations in this dataset" is the right one.

A separate and non-obvious finding is recorded in `perception/intent.py`:
**inflating covariance by classifier uncertainty does not make a prediction more
cautious.** Widening a distribution pushes its probability toward 0.5, so where the
mean trajectory already enters the corridor, more uncertainty *lowers* p_cross —
measured, 0.615 down to 0.570. `intent.py` therefore keeps `p_cross` honest and
exposes `p_cross_cautious` as the worst case over an ambiguity set, which is
monotone in uncertainty by construction.

## 6. Adversarial robustness and the sensor health monitor

`scripts/adversarial_test.py` injects ten sensor faults into recorded frames and
replays the full pipeline under each, asking two separate questions: how much does
the fault degrade the system, and **does the health monitor notice**. The second
question is the important one.

30 episodes, sampled across all four scenarios:

| Condition | Detections | pos RMSE | v_lat MAE | AUC | Camera health | Radar health | Noticed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean | 1,240 | 2.094 | 0.572 | 0.963 | 0.95 | 0.85 | — |
| darkness | 25 | 3.208 | 1.182 | — | 0.05 | 0.91 | YES |
| fog | 1,220 | 2.090 | 0.570 | 0.963 | 0.05 | 0.85 | YES |
| blur | 1,229 | 2.094 | 0.572 | 0.963 | 0.05 | 0.85 | YES |
| noise | 1,240 | 2.094 | 0.572 | 0.963 | 0.06 | 0.85 | YES |
| lens blocked | 799 | 2.148 | 0.482 | 0.951 | 0.38 | 0.88 | YES |
| radar dropout | 1,240 | 2.569 | 0.523 | 0.956 | 0.93 | 0.43 | YES |
| **radar clutter** | 1,240 | 2.378 | **0.702** | 0.953 | 0.95 | 0.91 | **NO** |
| **radar bias** | 1,240 | 2.194 | **0.752** | **0.910** | 0.95 | 0.85 | **NO** |
| extrinsic drift | 1,240 | 2.109 | 0.622 | 0.943 | 0.94 | 0.86 | NO |

Every camera fault is caught decisively — health drops from 0.95 to 0.05 under
darkness, fog, blur and noise, and to 0.38 with a partially blocked lens. Radar
dropout is caught (0.85 → 0.43).

**Two faults are silent failures, and this is the headline of this section.**
Radar clutter and radar bias both measurably degrade the system — lateral velocity
error rises 23% and 31% respectively, and bias costs 0.052 of intent AUC — while
the monitor continues to report the radar as healthy (0.91 and 0.85). Extrinsic
drift behaves the same way, costing 0.020 AUC unnoticed, though it is a geometric
fault rather than a sensor one and is not counted among the sensor failures.

This is the most dangerous failure category a system like this has: the pipeline
stays confident while being wrong, so nothing downstream has any reason to
discount its output.

### Why these two resist detection, and what it would take

We attempted to close this and got a precise answer instead of a fix, which is
worth reporting as a result in its own right.

**A fixed threshold cannot separate clutter from a busy scene.** The spatial
coherence check uses a Clark-Evans nearest-neighbour index, banded at 0.72–0.95.
Measured over 12 episodes, clean driving scores **0.383** and the injected
interference moves it to **0.499** — still below the 0.52 median of ordinary
driving, let alone the band where the check reacts. What counts as "coherent"
depends on the scene: an empty street and a junction full of parked cars differ
by more than the fault does.

`_radar_health` therefore gained a **relative** test, comparing scatter against
the sensor's own running median — the same principle the return-rate check
already used. It works, on the fault that matters: with clutter onsetting
mid-drive, radar health falls from **0.94 to 0.62** within about 60 frames, a
graded response downstream fusion can act on.

**But it still reports NO in the table above, and the reason is structural.**
The adversarial harness injects each fault from frame zero, so the monitor never
observes clean data. The baseline becomes the fault. *No self-referential monitor
can detect a constant fault that predates it* — there is nothing to be
inconsistent with. The same argument applies to range bias, and more strongly:
a constant offset on every return is fully absorbed by the filter, since an
object 2.5 m further away with the same bearing is a perfectly consistent world
state. The residuals return to zero once the track converges.

Detecting either from startup requires a reference the sensor cannot provide
itself — a factory calibration prior, or an independent metric range. The camera
offers the second: a pedestrian's pixel height against a known physical height
gives a coarse but *independent* range, and a persistent offset between that and
radar range is exactly the bias signature. That is the right next step, and it is
not implemented.

The honest summary for the report: **one of these faults is now detectable when
it onsets during operation, and neither is detectable when present from startup —
and the second half is a property of self-referential monitoring, not a gap in
the implementation.**

A methodological note worth carrying into the report: the first run of this test
selected episodes by alphabetical prefix, which returned 25 `blindspot_cutin`
episodes — a scenario containing no VRUs. Every metric came back empty, no fault
qualified as "degrading", and the script concluded that *every fault had been
detected*. The test now samples across scenarios and refuses to draw a conclusion
when the clean baseline tracked nothing. A robustness test that cannot fail is
worse than no robustness test, because it produces a reassuring number.

## 7. What changed from v1, and why the old numbers cannot be quoted

Four defects invalidated results computed from the 8,280-frame v1 dataset. All are
fixed in code, and v2 was collected fresh.

1. **Fully-occluded actors were deleted.** The collector dropped every box with
   `visibility < 0.15` — the hidden pedestrian, the entire subject of the project,
   never reached disk. This is the direct cause of the flat uncertainty
   calibration curve.
2. **The scenarios never produced occlusion anyway.** Walkers spawned 3 m to the
   *side* of the occluder at the same longitudinal station — zero depth
   separation. Measured across the full jitter range, the best case was 21%
   obscured and most were fully visible.
3. **Weather never applied.** 2,160 frames tagged `heavy_rain_night_fog` are bright
   clear-day images. Root cause confirmed on the live server: **CARLA 0.10.0's
   weather API is non-functional on this build.** `get_weather()` returns all-zero
   fields for every preset, and an alternating A/B with a 40-tick settle gives
   ClearNoon vs HardRainNight brightness deltas of −3.0, −3.1 and +0.3 points,
   where a real night scene is 100+ points darker. The collection code was never at
   fault; the simulator ignored every request silently. `_apply_weather` now raises
   `WeatherNotApplied` rather than writing mislabelled frames, and adverse-condition
   robustness is instead evaluated by applying calibrated degradations to captured
   frames (`scripts/adversarial_test.py`).
4. **85% of blind-spot frames were empty.** The cut-in spawned 12 m behind closing
   at 2.78 m/s, so it sat behind the camera for the first 40 of 60 recorded ticks
   — and it was the scenario's only actor. Now 14%.

**Every one of these produced well-formed files.** Valid images, valid labels,
valid grids, plausible statistics. That is the methodological lesson worth putting
in the report: a data pipeline fails silently by default, and only an explicit
gate catches it. `scripts/check_dataset.py` now encodes one assertion per defect
above and must pass before any collection is used.

Two further defects were caught by that gate during the v2 collection itself:

- **The roaming ego was parking itself.** The encounter manager parked its occluder
  1.6 m from lane centre; Town10HD_Opt lanes are ~3.5 m wide and the occluder
  blueprints are 2.5–2.8 m across, so the staged bus straddled the ego's own lane.
  The autopilot queued behind it and never moved again. Measured on the first
  collection: the ego was below 0.1 m/s for **79%** of urban frames, every episode
  ended still in the `triggered` phase, and 3,713 frames yielded **3** usable
  crossing observations. After the fix: 18% stationary, 5.84 m/s mean. Those
  frames were quarantined to `data/raw_v2_stalled_urban/` rather than used.
- **The blind-spot lane ran out mid-episode.** The spawn check verified an adjacent
  lane existed at the spawn point but not that it survived the ~60 m the ego then
  drove, so episodes aborted mid-run; one collection chunk delivered 42 frames of a
  300-frame target. The check now walks the lane forward before committing.

## 8. Performance and operational findings

**Measured end-to-end FPS: 2–9**, improving to **5.5–6.0** in the live dashboard
after MiDaS was moved off the per-frame path (`DEPTH_EVERY = 3` in
`scripts/live_demo.py`; `classify_grid` accepts a cached disparity). Depth changes
far more slowly than radar does, and at city speed the scene shifts well under one
2 m grid cell between refreshes, so radar still folds in every frame. Original
figures below in the live dashboard (YOLOv8n + evidential head
+ MiDaS + occlusion grid + CARLA UE5.5 rendering, all sharing one RTX 5060). The
specification's "MiDaS runs 15+ FPS" figure was for MiDaS alone on an RTX 3050.
The gap is dominated by UE5.5's rendering overhead and by running four models
concurrently on one GPU, not by MiDaS. Report the measured end-to-end number.

**The CARLA 0.10.0 server degrades severely with uptime.** Measured on the same
scene with the same code:

| Condition | FPS |
|---|---|
| Fresh server, no traffic | 14.1 |
| Fresh server, 30 vehicles + 15 walkers | 9.8 |
| Same server after ~20 min of spawn/destroy cycles | **0.17** |

An 80× slowdown, and it is *not* caused by the traffic — at the degraded point,
zero traffic and full traffic both measured 0.17 FPS. It is the server process.
Leftover actors accumulate too: a "clean" world after several killed runs held 128
actors. A restart restores full speed immediately.

Left unattended, a 15,000-frame run therefore starts near 10 FPS, decays toward
0.2, and takes many hours. `scripts/collect_chunked.py` collects in 1,500-frame
chunks with a fresh server per chunk; the full 15,420-frame v2 collection ran at a
**steady 4.8–8.1 frames/s across eight chunks** with no degradation, completing in
about 45 minutes.

**File handles are a real constraint at this dataset size.** `np.load` on an
`.npz` returns a lazy handle that stays open; holding one per frame across 15,420
frames exhausts the process limit and kills the run with `OSError [Errno 24]`.
This surfaced independently in three scripts, because the lazy object looks and
behaves exactly like a dict at the call site. `common/npz_io.load_npz` reads the
arrays eagerly and closes the file.

## 9. Honest summary of limitations

- **The BEV occlusion detector still under-detects**: recall 0.629 at the default
  operating point (0.854 at the recall-maximising end, at precision 0.483), so
  roughly two occluded cells in five are missed. Much improved — it was 0.259 —
  but under-detection remains its failure mode. §3.
- **No adverse-weather data exists**, and cannot be collected on this CARLA build.
  Robustness is evaluated by post-hoc frame degradation instead (§6), which is a weaker
  claim than simulating the conditions.
- **The evidential coupling to prediction is unproven in aggregate** — not
  contradicted, but the regime where it acts is 2% of observations here. §5.
- **Radar clutter is detectable only once it onsets mid-drive** (health 0.94 →
  0.62); **radar bias is not detectable at all** without an independent range
  reference. A fault present from startup becomes the monitor's own baseline. §6.
- **End-to-end throughput is 2–9 FPS**, below the 15+ FPS the specification
  targeted. §8.
- **Single town.** CARLA 0.10.0 ships only `Town10HD_Opt`, so every result is from
  one map's road geometry and building layout. Generalisation across towns is
  untested and untestable on this build.
- **Simulation only.** No claim is made about transfer to real sensors; MiDaS on
  synthetic imagery and CARLA's idealised radar are both friendlier than reality.
