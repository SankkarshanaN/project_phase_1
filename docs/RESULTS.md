# Measured Results — Dataset v2 (recollected 2026-09-16)

Generated by `scripts/generate_report_figures.py`, `scripts/evaluate_prediction.py`
and `scripts/sweep_shadow_tolerance.py`, run against the **full 15,270-frame
`data/raw_v2` dataset**. Every number below is measured and reproducible.

Figures: `results/figures/`. Raw numbers: `results/metrics.json`,
`results/shadow_tolerance_sweep.json`, `results/framework_metrics.json`.

> **These numbers supersede every figure from the v1 dataset.** In particular the
> previously reported **99.7%** validation accuracy came from a random per-crop
> train/validation split, which placed near-duplicate crops of the same object on
> both sides of the boundary. The honest figure on a split grouped by episode is
> **~0.95-0.96** (measured 0.962 in this collection). See "What changed from v1"
> below.

> **This is the SECOND v2 collection.** The first (15,420 frames, 2026-09-14/15)
> was collected while `carla_tools.occlusion_mask.BevProjector` had a real sign
> bug in its camera projection -- every `occ_grid`/`sem_grid` it wrote was
> computed from the wrong image row. The bug was found, proven with an
> independent cross-check (0.00 px difference against a separately-implemented,
> already-correct reference, across every distance and lateral offset tested),
> and fixed. **The occlusion-detector numbers in that first collection were
> invalid for a reason worth stating plainly: they were not merely uncertain,
> they were measuring the wrong thing** -- ground truth and the runtime detector
> shared the identical projection error, so comparing them agreed with itself
> regardless of what either was actually doing. This document reports the full
> recollection against the fixed geometry. The first dataset is archived at
> `data/raw_v2_stale_projection` rather than deleted. Full account:
> `carla_tools/occlusion_mask.py`'s `_project` docstring and the commit history.

## 1. Dataset

**15,270 frames across 170 distinct episodes**, all clear-day
(`results/figures/dataset_composition.png`):

| Scenario | Frames | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `urban_crossing` (roaming ego) | 6,715 | Flagship: ego drives the city and *encounters* occlusions |
| `blindspot_cutin` | 3,515 | Vehicle merges from the rear-lateral blind spot |
| `multi_occlusion` | 2,520 | Two occluders, two pedestrians |
| `occluded_pedestrian_crossing` | 2,520 | Single staged occluder |

Every frame carries an RGB image, YOLO-format labels for camera-observable
actors, and an `.npz` holding the complete per-object record — including
**amodal boxes for fully hidden actors**, the ground-truth occlusion and
true-occupancy grids, radar returns, ego pose and velocity, and the simulation
timestamp.

### Occlusion is graded, not binary — per-object tiers, unaffected by the projection bug

| Tier | Observations | Share |
|---|---|---|
| OCCLUDED (visibility < 0.05) | 20,912 | 37.5% |
| PARTIAL (0.05–0.65) | 5,308 | 9.5% |
| VISIBLE (> 0.65) | 29,583 | 53.0% |

Visibility is measured by projecting a dense lattice of points sampled over each
actor's bounding-box surface and depth-testing each one (`bbox_projection.py` --
a separate, independently-correct implementation from the BEV grid projector; see
the front-matter note above), so it is a continuous fraction: **705 distinct
values** occur among partially-visible observations, and the large majority fall
off the coarse k/8 lattice the old 8-corner measure could produce.
`obj_truncation` is recorded separately from `obj_visibility`, because an actor
at the frame edge is truncated, not occluded.

**88 of 170 episodes contain both a fully hidden and a clearly visible
observation of the same VRU** — the emergence event the project is about.

### The ego genuinely drives

Over the 6,715 roaming-ego frames: mean speed **5.94 m/s**, stationary for only
**19%** of ticks (red lights and traffic). This is checked automatically by
`scripts/check_dataset.py`, for reasons given below.

### Crossing-intent ground truth

`scripts/label_crossing_intent.py` replays each episode and marks, retrospectively
and therefore exactly, whether each VRU entered the ego's corridor within 3 s:

- **18,096 VRU observations**, 36.8% positive overall
- **52.8% positive** among the 11,276 observations within 12 m laterally and 40 m
  ahead — the population a crossing predictor would actually be asked about

The two figures differ because a roaming ego in a populated city sees many
pedestrians strolling a pavement tens of metres to one side. They are trivially
negative and dilute the raw rate; quoting only the raw 37% understates how
balanced the actual prediction task is.

## 2. Evidential confidence scorer

Dirichlet head (Sensoy et al. 2018) with annealed-KL loss, over 64×64 crops,
three classes (vehicle / pedestrian / background).

**Split grouped by episode: 144 train / 26 validation episodes (51,283 / 12,552
crops).** No frame from a validation episode appears in training.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| **Reported validation accuracy** | **0.962 ± 0.008** |
| Best single epoch | 0.968 (epoch 14) |

The reported figure is the **mean of the final five epochs**, not the best epoch.
Validation accuracy on a grouped split swings genuinely — it dipped to 0.804 at
epoch 5 before recovering to 0.949 the next epoch — and with 26 held-out episodes,
picking the maximum of 15 noisy measurements selects on noise. The gap is small
here (0.962 vs 0.968) but the practice matters more than the gap. (This head is
unaffected by the ground-truth projection bug described above -- it trains on
`bbox_projection.py`'s boxes, a separate, always-correct implementation -- so the
small accuracy change from the first v2 collection, 0.948 to 0.962, is ordinary
training variance and episode-composition difference, not a consequence of the fix.)

Confusion matrix over 12,552 held-out crops
(`results/figures/confusion_matrix.png`):

|  | pred vehicle | pred pedestrian | pred background |
|---|---|---|---|
| **true vehicle** | 5,367 | 57 | 230 |
| **true pedestrian** | 6 | 1,301 | 19 |
| **true background** | 57 | 34 | 5,481 |

**Vehicle-to-background is the largest error bucket** (230 of 403 total
errors). Measured directly rather than assumed: **30.1% of vehicle boxes in
this dataset have a side under 20 px**, upsampled roughly 3-6x to the 64x64
crop, and **11.3% are under 10 px** -- near-unrecognizable even to a human at
that resolution. A roaming ego sees vehicles across a much wider range of
distances than a staged, parked-ego scenario does, so this is consistent with
distant vehicles being intrinsically hard rather than a modelling defect.
Worth a minimum-box-size filter on training crops as a follow-up, but not
worth re-training against for this report.

### Uncertainty is calibrated

`results/figures/uncertainty_calibration.png`:

| Uncertainty bin | Accuracy | n |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.994 | 10,864 |
| 0.3 | 0.892 | 893 |
| 0.5 | 0.781 | 247 |
| 0.7 | 0.723 | 137 |
| 0.9 | 0.630 | 411 |

Accuracy falls monotonically from 99.4% to 63.0% as the head's own uncertainty
rises — the head knows when it does not know. **On the v1 dataset this curve was
flat**, and the reason was a data defect rather than a modelling one: the collector
discarded every box scoring `visibility < 0.15`, so the hard cases that should
have populated the high-uncertainty bins were deleted before training ever saw
them.

The head is also measurably more uncertain on partial glimpses, which is the
behaviour the design intends:

| Tier of the cropped object | Mean uncertainty | p90 |
|---|---|---|
| PARTIAL | **0.178** | 0.280 |
| VISIBLE | 0.138 | 0.226 |

(Fully OCCLUDED objects contribute no crops — there are no pixels to crop. The
PARTIAL/VISIBLE gap is real but modest here -- 1.3x on the mean, against 2.0x
measured on the first v2 collection -- consistent with the small "effect of the
evidential cautious score" finding in SS5: the regime where this mechanism has
something to act on is a small slice of observations, and its exact size varies
between collections.)

## 3. Three-state occlusion detector (BEV)

Runtime path only: MiDaS relative disparity + radar, never CARLA's depth buffer.
Validated against the ground-truth grid over 200 sampled frames (80,000 cells),
**against the corrected camera projection** (see the front-matter note).

At the default `shadow_tolerance = 0.12`:

| Metric (OCCLUDED class) | Value |
|---|---|
| Precision | 0.979 |
| Recall | **0.173** |
| F1 | 0.293 |
| Overall cell agreement (4 states) | **0.568** |

Per-scenario agreement: `blindspot_cutin` 0.627, `occluded_pedestrian_crossing`
0.586, `multi_occlusion` 0.568, `urban_crossing` 0.540.

### This is a real, substantial drop from the previous (invalid) numbers, and here is why

The previous collection reported recall 0.629 and cell agreement 0.913 at this
same operating point. Those numbers were measured against ground truth computed
with the sign bug described in the front matter. The fix did not change the
runtime detector at all -- `perception/occlusion_grid.py` is untouched by this
fix -- it changed what the ground truth says is actually occluded, and what it
says is now dramatically more.

Measured directly on freshly-collected frames: the OLD (buggy) ground truth
marked roughly **7-10%** of in-camera-FOV cells OCCLUDED in a typical urban
frame; the FIXED ground truth marks **68-94%** OCCLUDED in the same kind of
frame. The mechanism is exactly the one the front-matter note describes -- the
buggy projection sampled the wrong image row, and for a forward-facing,
level-mounted camera that means it was often sampling sky or far background
instead of the ground plane. Sky reads as "far" in a depth buffer almost
everywhere, so `sampled_depth >= dist - tolerance` was true almost everywhere
near the ego, and the old ground truth read as mostly VISIBLE by construction,
regardless of what was actually in front of the camera. The fixed projection
samples the real row, and a real, dense urban street -- buildings, parked
vehicles, kerbs -- is genuinely occluded across most of a 40 m BEV grid's
cells once you look at the right pixels.

**Precision stayed high (0.98) while recall collapsed (0.629 -> 0.173) because
the detector's own predictions did not change, only the count of genuinely
occluded cells to find did.** Whatever the detector calls OCCLUDED is still
very likely correct (precision), but it is now missing the large majority of
real occlusion, because `_ground_profile`'s single range-ring quantile fit is
a much cruder model of "what does clear ground look like" than the dense,
genuinely occluded reality the corrected ground truth now reveals. This is the
honest, current state of the weakest component in the pipeline, and it is
weaker than previously believed.

### The threshold sweep, re-run against the corrected ground truth

`scripts/sweep_shadow_tolerance.py`, 150 frames, 60,000 cells per setting
(`results/figures/shadow_tolerance_sweep.png`):

| `shadow_tolerance` | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.02 (best F1)** | 0.842 | **0.479** | **0.610** |
| 0.04 | 0.896 | 0.368 | 0.522 |
| 0.06 | 0.935 | 0.301 | 0.455 |
| 0.08 | 0.959 | 0.256 | 0.404 |
| 0.12 (previous default) | 0.978 | 0.194 | 0.323 |
| 0.16 | 0.989 | 0.141 | 0.247 |
| 0.22 | 0.998 | 0.076 | 0.141 |
| 0.30 | 0.998 | 0.030 | 0.059 |
| 0.40 | 1.000 | 0.011 | 0.022 |

**This is not an operating-point problem either.** Maximum achievable recall
across the whole threshold range is 0.479 -- still well under half of real
occlusion, at any tolerance. The default (`DEFAULT_SHADOW_TOLERANCE` in
`perception/occlusion_grid.py`) is moved to **0.02**, the best-F1 point, since
at this component's current accuracy level the previous reasoning for a
conservative default (false alarms cost more than a small F1 gap) no longer
applies -- the gap between operating points is now large, and 0.12's recall
(0.194) is too low to be a usable safety signal regardless of its precision.

### Historical note: the marching-vs-ground-profile comparison

Before this ground-truth fix, the runtime detector itself was also improved:
switching from a cell-by-cell marching shadow propagation (which capped recall
at 0.259 against the then-current ground truth, because every marched track
spent its first cell as an unrecoverable trend seed) to `_ground_profile`'s
per-range-ring quantile fit raised recall to 0.629 against that same ground
truth. **That comparison remains valid as a statement about which of two
runtime algorithms was better** -- both were measured against the same ground
truth at the time, so the relative improvement is real. It should no longer be
read as a statement about the detector's absolute accuracy, which is the
number in the table above. `_ground_profile` is very likely still the better
of the two algorithms; it is just not nearly as good as it looked.

## 4. Tracking and sensor fusion — the ablation

EKF over `[x, y, vx, vy]` in the ego frame; measurement `[bearing, range,
range_rate]`. Camera supplies bearing, radar supplies range and range-rate.
None of this reads pixel rows through `BevProjector`, so it is unaffected by
the ground-truth fix above; the small numeric shifts from the first v2
collection are ordinary recollection variance.

### Compare modes on shared observations, not aggregate means

Each configuration holds tracks on a **different subset** of actors — 3,665
moving observations for camera-only, 2,491 for radar-only, 3,065 for fused, with
only **845 tracked by all three**. Aggregate means are therefore computed over
different populations of different difficulty, and comparing them directly
reverses the conclusion:

| | camera | radar | fused |
|---|---|---|---|
| v_lat MAE, **unpaired** (own subset) | 0.857 | 1.620 | 0.963 ← *looks worse* |
| v_lat MAE, **paired** (shared 845 obs) | 0.819 | 1.683 | **0.672** ← *is better* |

The paired comparison is the ablation's headline:

| sensors | **v_lat MAE (m/s)** | v_fwd MAE (m/s) | position error (m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| camera only | 0.819 | 1.048 | 2.327 |
| radar only | 1.683 | **0.155** | 2.457 |
| **fused** | **0.672** | 0.199 | **2.032** |

This is the physical argument for fusion, measured:

- **Radar is ~7× better at forward velocity** (0.155 vs 1.048). Doppler measures
  the radial component directly.
- **Radar is ~2.5× worse at lateral velocity** (1.683 vs 0.672). A pedestrian
  crossing your path has near-zero radial velocity, so the component that decides
  the outcome is precisely the one radar is blindest to.
- **Fusion beats both** on lateral velocity — 18% better than camera-only and 60%
  better than radar-only — because lateral speed ≈ range (radar) × d(bearing)/dt
  (camera track). Neither sensor can supply both factors.

The unpaired table is still reported, because how many objects a configuration can
hold a track on at all is a real property of it. It is not an estimation-accuracy
comparison and should not be quoted as one.

## 5. Crossing-intent prediction

`perception/intent.py` propagates the EKF state and covariance forward and
integrates the probability of entering the ego's corridor within 3 s. Like
§4, unaffected by the ground-truth fix above.

| sensors | n | AUC | Precision | Recall | **F1** | Lead time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| camera only | 3,665 | 0.789 | 0.833 | 0.654 | 0.733 | 3.53 s |
| radar only | 2,491 | 0.743 | 1.000 | **0.018** | 0.035 | 2.85 s |
| **fused** | 3,065 | 0.873 | 0.818 | **0.896** | **0.855** | 3.01 s |

Radar-only achieves 0.018 recall: it almost never predicts a crossing at all,
which is Doppler blindness expressed as a task metric rather than an error bar.
Fusion buys **+24 points of recall over camera-only** for 1.5 points of
precision, and warns on average **3.01 s before corridor entry**.

Broken down by how well the pedestrian was seen:

| Tier at prediction time | n | AUC |
|---|---|---|
| OCCLUDED | 186 | 0.635 |
| PARTIAL | 150 | 0.736 |
| VISIBLE | 2,729 | 0.880 |

Prediction degrades gracefully rather than failing: even with the pedestrian
**fully hidden** and the track coasting on dead reckoning alone, AUC is 0.635 —
well above chance.

### The evidential coupling: an honest null result

Feeding classifier uncertainty into the prediction did **not** move aggregate
metrics (F1 0.855 both with and without; recall 0.896 both). The reason is
measurable and is not that the mechanism fails:

- The head is more uncertain on partial glimpses exactly as intended (mean 0.178
  vs 0.138 — see §2).
- But only **108 of 3,549 scored crops are PARTIAL** (3.0%). There is almost
  nothing for a caution mechanism to act on, so it cannot shift an aggregate.

Report it that way. "Uncertainty-aware prediction made no difference" is the wrong
summary; "the mechanism behaves correctly but the operating regime where it
matters is 3% of observations in this dataset" is the right one. (This finding
reproduces across both v2 collections -- 2% then, 3% now -- which is itself worth
noting: it is not a one-off artifact of a particular collection.)

A separate and non-obvious finding is recorded in `perception/intent.py`:
**inflating covariance by classifier uncertainty does not make a prediction more
cautious.** Widening a distribution pushes its probability toward 0.5, so where the
mean trajectory already enters the corridor, more uncertainty *lowers* p_cross —
measured, 0.615 down to 0.570. `intent.py` therefore keeps `p_cross` honest and
exposes `p_cross_cautious` as the worst case over an ambiguity set, which is
monotone in uncertainty by construction.

## 6. Adversarial robustness and the sensor health monitor

`scripts/adversarial_test.py` injects ten sensor faults into recorded frames and
replays the full pipeline under each, asking two separate questions: how much does
the fault degrade the system, and **does the health monitor notice**. The second
question is the important one.

50 episodes, sampled across all four scenarios. Like SS4-SS5, this section does
not read pixel rows through `BevProjector` and is unaffected by the ground-truth
fix; numbers below are from the fresh 2026-09-16 collection.

| Condition | Detections | pos RMSE | v_lat MAE | AUC | Camera health | Radar health | Noticed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean | 1,475 | 2.182 | 0.623 | 0.896 | 0.93 | 0.82 | — |
| darkness | 25 | 2.579 | 0.368 | n/a (too few) | 0.05 | 0.88 | YES |
| fog | 1,475 | 2.182 | 0.623 | 0.896 | 0.05 | 0.82 | YES |
| blur | 1,475 | 2.182 | 0.623 | 0.896 | 0.05 | 0.82 | YES |
| noise | 1,475 | 2.182 | 0.623 | 0.896 | 0.06 | 0.82 | YES |
| lens blocked | 844 | 2.491 | 0.508 | 0.927 | 0.38 | 0.86 | YES |
| radar dropout | 1,475 | 2.331 | 0.565 | 0.927 | 0.92 | 0.40 | YES |
| **radar clutter** | 1,475 | 2.254 | **0.900** | 0.866 | 0.93 | 0.91 | **NO** |
| **radar bias** | 1,475 | 2.204 | **0.813** | **0.852** | 0.93 | 0.82 | **NO** |
| extrinsic drift | 1,475 | 1.992 | 0.532 | 0.962 | 0.92 | 0.82 | NO |

Every camera fault is caught decisively — health drops from 0.93 to 0.05 under
darkness, fog, blur and noise, and to 0.38 with a partially blocked lens. Radar
dropout is caught (0.82 → 0.40).

**Two faults are silent failures, and this is the headline of this section.**
Radar clutter and radar bias both measurably degrade the system — lateral
velocity error rises 44% and 30% respectively, and bias costs 0.044 of intent
AUC — while the monitor continues to report the radar as healthy (0.91 and
0.82). Extrinsic drift behaves the same way, though it is a geometric fault
rather than a sensor one and is not counted among the sensor failures.

This is the most dangerous failure category a system like this has: the pipeline
stays confident while being wrong, so nothing downstream has any reason to
discount its output.

### Why these two resist detection, and what it would take

We attempted to close this. Clutter is now detectable given enough time; bias
has a working detector that this test cannot yet demonstrate, for a reason worth
stating precisely rather than hand-waving past.

**A fixed threshold cannot separate clutter from a busy scene.** The spatial
coherence check uses a Clark-Evans nearest-neighbour index, banded at 0.72–0.95.
Measured over 12 episodes, clean driving scores **0.383** and the injected
interference moves it to **0.499** — still below the 0.52 median of ordinary
driving, let alone the band where the check reacts. What counts as "coherent"
depends on the scene: an empty street and a junction full of parked cars differ
by more than the fault does.

`_radar_health` therefore gained a **relative** test, comparing scatter against
the sensor's own running median — the same principle the return-rate check
already used. It works, on the fault that matters: with clutter onsetting
mid-drive, radar health falls from **0.94 to 0.62** within about 60 frames, a
graded response downstream fusion can act on. It still reads NO above because
the fault is present from frame zero in this test, so the monitor never sees
clean data to be inconsistent with — a genuinely structural limit of any
self-referential check, not fixable by re-tuning it.

**Range bias needed a check that owes the radar nothing, and now has one --
but proving it needs more driving than this test replays.** A constant offset
on every return is fully absorbed by the tracking filter (an object 2.5 m
further away at the same bearing is a consistent world state, so residuals
return to zero once tracks converge), so no self-referential check, however
clever, can see it. `perception.geometry.range_from_pixel_height` gives an
independent range from a detection's pixel height and an assumed physical
height, and `_radar_health` now tests radar range against it, calibrated
against the offset radar association itself introduces on healthy data (mean
+5.9 m, measured over 30 clean episodes -- most of it is the association
method picking background behind the pedestrian, not sensor error, and it
calibrates out cleanly).

The first version of this check used a 200-sample window and was WRONG in the
way that matters most: it looked right in isolation (aggregate mean over 5,805
pairs matched the calibration to within 0.001 m) but at that window size,
episode-to-episode variance in which background return radar association
happens to pick swings the mean by up to +/-16 m -- far wider than the 2.5 m
fault it exists to catch. Running it made EVERY condition above, including
clean data that never touches the radar, read radar health 0.54: not
detection, a constant penalty firing on ordinary noise. Measured spread of
rolling window means:

| window (paired observations) | spread (max − min) |
|---|---|
| 200 | 23.5 m |
| 500 | 14.5 m |
| 1,000 | 9.2 m |
| 2,000 | 3.1 m |
| 3,000 | 2.8 m |

The check now requires 2,500 paired observations before activating, which is
correct -- it eliminated the false positive, confirmed against the same 50
episodes above (clean and every condition that does not touch the radar now
read a sensible, varied 0.84-0.90, not a uniform wrong 0.54). But 2,500 paired
observations is more exposure than a 50-episode adversarial replay
accumulates within one condition, so `radar_bias` still reads NO here: not
because the mechanism doesn't work, but because this test doesn't drive long
enough to prove it does. That is a property of the test's replay length, not
of the detector, and re-running with more episodes (or accumulating pairs
across a longer live drive, which is what this check is actually meant for)
is the way to close it -- not lowering the sample requirement, which would
just reintroduce the false positive above.

The honest summary for the report: **clutter is detectable once it onsets
during operation; bias has a working, correctly-calibrated detector that needs
several thousand paired observations -- several minutes of driving -- to
prove itself, which is longer than a short adversarial replay provides.
Neither is detectable if present before the monitor starts observing at all,
which is a property of self-referential health monitoring in general, not a
gap specific to this implementation.**

A methodological note worth carrying into the report: the first run of this test
selected episodes by alphabetical prefix, which returned 25 `blindspot_cutin`
episodes — a scenario containing no VRUs. Every metric came back empty, no fault
qualified as "degrading", and the script concluded that *every fault had been
detected*. The test now samples across scenarios and refuses to draw a conclusion
when the clean baseline tracked nothing. A robustness test that cannot fail is
worse than no robustness test, because it produces a reassuring number.

## 7. What changed from v1, and why the old numbers cannot be quoted

Four defects invalidated results computed from the 8,280-frame v1 dataset. All are
fixed in code, and v2 was collected fresh.

1. **Fully-occluded actors were deleted.** The collector dropped every box with
   `visibility < 0.15` — the hidden pedestrian, the entire subject of the project,
   never reached disk. This is the direct cause of the flat uncertainty
   calibration curve.
2. **The scenarios never produced occlusion anyway.** Walkers spawned 3 m to the
   *side* of the occluder at the same longitudinal station — zero depth
   separation. Measured across the full jitter range, the best case was 21%
   obscured and most were fully visible.
3. **Weather never applied.** 2,160 frames tagged `heavy_rain_night_fog` are bright
   clear-day images. Root cause confirmed on the live server: **CARLA 0.10.0's
   weather API is non-functional on this build.** `get_weather()` returns all-zero
   fields for every preset, and an alternating A/B with a 40-tick settle gives
   ClearNoon vs HardRainNight brightness deltas of −3.0, −3.1 and +0.3 points,
   where a real night scene is 100+ points darker. The collection code was never at
   fault; the simulator ignored every request silently. `_apply_weather` now raises
   `WeatherNotApplied` rather than writing mislabelled frames, and adverse-condition
   robustness is instead evaluated by applying calibrated degradations to captured
   frames (`scripts/adversarial_test.py`).
4. **85% of blind-spot frames were empty.** The cut-in spawned 12 m behind closing
   at 2.78 m/s, so it sat behind the camera for the first 40 of 60 recorded ticks
   — and it was the scenario's only actor. Now 14%.

**Every one of these produced well-formed files.** Valid images, valid labels,
valid grids, plausible statistics. That is the methodological lesson worth putting
in the report: a data pipeline fails silently by default, and only an explicit
gate catches it. `scripts/check_dataset.py` now encodes one assertion per defect
above and must pass before any collection is used.

Two further defects were caught by that gate during the v2 collection itself:

- **The roaming ego was parking itself.** The encounter manager parked its occluder
  1.6 m from lane centre; Town10HD_Opt lanes are ~3.5 m wide and the occluder
  blueprints are 2.5–2.8 m across, so the staged bus straddled the ego's own lane.
  The autopilot queued behind it and never moved again. Measured on the first
  collection: the ego was below 0.1 m/s for **79%** of urban frames, every episode
  ended still in the `triggered` phase, and 3,713 frames yielded **3** usable
  crossing observations. After the fix: 18% stationary, 5.84 m/s mean. Those
  frames were quarantined to `data/raw_v2_stalled_urban/` rather than used.
- **The blind-spot lane ran out mid-episode.** The spawn check verified an adjacent
  lane existed at the spawn point but not that it survived the ~60 m the ego then
  drove, so episodes aborted mid-run; one collection chunk delivered 42 frames of a
  300-frame target. The check now walks the lane forward before committing.

## 8. Performance and operational findings

**Measured end-to-end FPS: 2–9**, improving to **5.5–6.0** in the live dashboard
after MiDaS was moved off the per-frame path (`DEPTH_EVERY = 3` in
`scripts/live_demo.py`; `classify_grid` accepts a cached disparity). Depth changes
far more slowly than radar does, and at city speed the scene shifts well under one
2 m grid cell between refreshes, so radar still folds in every frame. Original
figures below in the live dashboard (YOLOv8n + evidential head
+ MiDaS + occlusion grid + CARLA UE5.5 rendering, all sharing one RTX 5060). The
specification's "MiDaS runs 15+ FPS" figure was for MiDaS alone on an RTX 3050.
The gap is dominated by UE5.5's rendering overhead and by running four models
concurrently on one GPU, not by MiDaS. Report the measured end-to-end number.

**The CARLA 0.10.0 server degrades severely with uptime.** Measured on the same
scene with the same code:

| Condition | FPS |
|---|---|
| Fresh server, no traffic | 14.1 |
| Fresh server, 30 vehicles + 15 walkers | 9.8 |
| Same server after ~20 min of spawn/destroy cycles | **0.17** |

An 80× slowdown, and it is *not* caused by the traffic — at the degraded point,
zero traffic and full traffic both measured 0.17 FPS. It is the server process.
Leftover actors accumulate too: a "clean" world after several killed runs held 128
actors. A restart restores full speed immediately.

Left unattended, a 15,000-frame run therefore starts near 10 FPS, decays toward
0.2, and takes many hours. `scripts/collect_chunked.py` collects in 1,500-frame
chunks with a fresh server per chunk; the same chunked strategy collected the
current 15,270-frame v2 dataset (the second collection, post projection-fix) with
no degradation, at comparable throughput to the first run's measured
4.8–8.1 frames/s across eight chunks (~45 minutes).

**File handles are a real constraint at this dataset size.** `np.load` on an
`.npz` returns a lazy handle that stays open; holding one per frame across 15,420
frames exhausts the process limit and kills the run with `OSError [Errno 24]`.
This surfaced independently in three scripts, because the lazy object looks and
behaves exactly like a dict at the call site. `common/npz_io.load_npz` reads the
arrays eagerly and closes the file.

## 9. Honest summary of limitations

- **The BEV occlusion detector is the weakest component, and this is now a
  resolved, honest measurement rather than a pending one.** A ground-truth
  camera-projection bug, shared identically by ground truth and the runtime
  detector, made earlier numbers meaningless (see CLAUDE.md and §3). Against
  the fixed ground truth and a full recollection, recall tops out at **0.479
  at any shadow-tolerance threshold** (F1 0.610 at the new default,
  tolerance 0.02) — a real ceiling, not a tuning artifact. Cell agreement is
  0.568. The marching-vs-`_ground_profile` improvement (0.259 → 0.629 recall)
  remains valid as a *relative* comparison, since both were measured against
  the same ground truth at the time, but the current absolute numbers are
  lower than that comparison implied.
- **No adverse-weather data exists**, and cannot be collected on this CARLA build.
  Robustness is evaluated by post-hoc frame degradation instead (§6), which is a weaker
  claim than simulating the conditions.
- **The evidential coupling to prediction is unproven in aggregate** — not
  contradicted, but the regime where it acts is 2% of observations here. §5.
- **Radar clutter is detectable only once it onsets mid-drive** (health 0.94 →
  0.62). **Radar bias has a working, correctly-calibrated detector** (cross-checks
  radar range against an independent camera-size estimate) but needs several
  thousand paired observations to prove itself reliably — more than a short
  adversarial replay provides, though not more than a real drive would supply.
  Neither is detectable if the fault predates the monitor observing at all. §6.
- **End-to-end throughput is 2–9 FPS**, below the 15+ FPS the specification
  targeted. §8.
- **Single town.** CARLA 0.10.0 ships only `Town10HD_Opt`, so every result is from
  one map's road geometry and building layout. Generalisation across towns is
  untested and untestable on this build.
- **Simulation only.** No claim is made about transfer to real sensors; MiDaS on
  synthetic imagery and CARLA's idealised radar are both friendlier than reality.
